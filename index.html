READING THE DATA

A transition is more than a percentage.

A source can lose share while still generating more electricity in absolute terms, if total demand grows faster. To understand the transition, compare both generation in TWh and each source's share of the total.

Data: Ember, Yearly Electricity Data (electricity statistics); Our World in Data energy dataset for the year-by-year maps; Natural Earth country boundaries for world maps; Malaysia Energy Commission major-power-station maps (2021) for selected Malaysian stations. Station markers are approximate. Tile-map positions are simplified to reduce overlap. Latest year is selected from the data available for each view. Source categories and definitions follow the dataset. This page is an exploratory student visualisation, not an official forecast.

Methodology and limitations

Electricity generation is measured in terawatt-hours (TWh), while generation shares are expressed as percentages. Absolute generation and percentage share describe different things: a source's share may decline even while its output increases.

Country comparisons depend on data availability, reporting periods and the definitions used by the source dataset. Missing values should not be interpreted as zero generation. Power-station locations are approximate where stated, and the selected stations do not represent a complete national inventory.

Source links accessed 16 October 2026. Dataset coverage and publication dates may change as providers update their data. All charts should be interpreted using the year, unit and category definitions displayed with each figure.
